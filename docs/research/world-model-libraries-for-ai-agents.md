## Executive summary

**At a glance**

- No library today lets an AI agent discover, plug in, parameterize, compose, query and simulate a component-level "world model" of a faucet or a pump as a portable object; the physics exists without the packaging.

No library today lets an AI agent discover, plug in, parameterize, compose, query and simulate a component-level "world model" of a faucet or a pump as a first-class, portable object; what exists is the physics without the packaging. The equations for both examples already ship in open, actively maintained libraries: the Modelica Buildings Library 13.0.0 (4 May 2026, BSD-3) carries ten two- and three-way valves with Kv/Cv sizing, `SpeedControlled_y` and the other `Fluid.Movers` models with **18 manufacturer-sourced Wilo pump records**, and a PI-controlled `ThermostaticMixingValve` ([LBNL](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/library-release/2026/05/04/releases-11.1.1-12.1.1-13.0.0.html)); WNTR 1.5.0 and EPANET 2.3.5 give a pump as a head curve inside a JSON-serializable network ([USEPA/WNTR](https://github.com/USEPA/WNTR)); and WaterTAP 1.7.0 gives 115 zero-order treatment units plus rigorous RO, UV-AOP and pump models ([WaterTAP](https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/technical_reference/unit_models/index.html)).

What none of them provides is an agent-readable manifest (ports with medium and causality, parameters with units and validity ranges, canonical test scenarios), a lightweight runtime that runs inside a 3D scene or a plant-analysis loop, a registry to find such components, or a validation layer that catches "executable but physically wrong" models. The evidence that this packaging is the bottleneck is consistent across 2025–2026 studies: GPT-4o writes Modelica that loads 95.56% of the time but is functionally correct only **24.44%** of the time ([ModiGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18460)), ten LLMs reach **0.0 simulation fidelity** on fluid-system code ([arXiv 2607.29389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29389)), yet an agent hits **100% task success** when constrained to typed tools over a pre-built simulator ([EPANET-Agentic, Water Research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135426001156); [GitHub](https://github.com/wangjian169/EPANET-Agentic)).

The user's idea corresponds to the oldest of three things now called "world models" (explicit, equation- or rule-based component models), not to the learned video/latent models that dominate the 2026 vocabulary, and the credible first market is the pump-in-a-plant case (water utilities and consultants with paying constituencies and an open Python substrate), not the bathroom faucet (no commercial evidence found). The recommended path is to wrap rather than build: publish a component-manifest convention over FMI/SSP and Modelica, ship a small robust-by-default catalogue of pump and valve components composed at the Modelica level and compiled to FMUs behind an MCP server, validate it on a water-network benchmark, and partner for manufacturer curve data rather than digitizing it alone.

## "World model" now means three things, and yours is the oldest one

### The three families

The phrase covers at least three families that share almost nothing except the name, and the confusion matters because searching for a "world model library" returns the wrong family. The first is the explicit, symbolic family: equation-based component models with typed ports (Modelica, ModelingToolkit, Simscape), packaged executables (FMI/FMU, SSP), discrete formalisms (PDDL, statecharts/SCXML, DEVS, system dynamics) and causal or probabilistic programs. Here a faucet is a class with `Kv`, an `opening` input in 0..1, a `leakageOpening` to avoid structural singularity and a regularization of **1% of `dp_nominal`** around zero flow, following IEC 534 / ISA S.75 ([PartialValve](https://doc.modelica.org/Modelica%204.0.0/Resources/helpOM/Modelica.Fluid.Valves.BaseClasses.PartialValve.html)).

The second family is descriptive digital-twin semantics: DTDL, the Asset Administration Shell, OPC UA companion specs, W3C WoT Thing Descriptions, Brick, IFC/bSDD, SAREF, Matter and NGSI-LD. Every one of these describes structure, identity, properties, telemetry and sometimes commands and state enumerations; none defines executable dynamics, and only two standardized bridges from description to simulation exist, the IDTA 02005 "Provision of Simulation Models" AAS submodel that points at FMI/Modelica/Simulink files and describes their ports and causality, and the FIWARE Smart Data Models EPANET entities, whose `Pump` entity carries `headCurve` and `efficCurve` and which define a separate `SimulationScenario` entity carrying solver settings and a required `hasInputNetwork` link ([IDTA 02005](https://industrialdigitaltwin.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IDTA-02005-1-0_Submodel_ProvisionOfSimulationModels.pdf); [Smart Data Models Pump](https://github.com/smart-data-models/dataModel.WaterDistributionManagementEPANET/blob/master/Pump/doc/spec.md); [SimulationScenario](https://github.com/smart-data-models/dataModel.WaterDistributionManagementEPANET/blob/master/SimulationScenario/doc/spec.md)).

The third family is the learned, neural one that the AI research community means in 2026: latent-dynamics models for planning (DreamerV3, V-JEPA 2-AC), action-conditioned generative video (Genie 3, Cosmos 3, Runway GWM-1), generative 3D scene models (World Labs Marble and Atlas), and purpose-trained LLMs predicting the next state of a software environment (CWM, Qwen-AgentWorld). LLM-written programmatic world models (WorldCoder, PoE-World) sit between the families rather than inside the third: the LLM authors a transition program, but a deterministic program does the predicting, which is the pattern of the explicit family with the LLM as author. A 26-author May 2026 survey organizes the learned family along four axes and names PlaNet, Dreamer, MuZero, Sora, Cosmos and Genie as milestones ([arXiv 2606.00133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00133)).

### Where the faucet and pump belong

The user's faucet and pump belong squarely to the first family, and the other two relate to it as neighbours rather than substitutes. Learned models are monolithic functions from scene and action to the next observation or latent, trained per domain; none exposes objects as separately addressable, parameterizable units, and none carries quantities with units. The World Labs World API returns "explorable 3D worlds" from text or images over HTTP and integrates with Isaac Sim and MuJoCo, but describes no physics or behaviour API ([World Labs](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/announcing-the-world-api)); Cosmos 3 (31 May 2026) ships open weights under OpenMDW 1.1 in 65B/16B/4B variants and is positioned for synthetic data and policy training, not for state variables ([NVIDIA press release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-launches-cosmos-3-the-open-frontier-foundation-model-for-physical-ai); [nvidia.com/cosmos](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/); [HF collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/cosmos3)).

The benchmarks confirm that what these models measure is plausibility, not correctness: the best score on Physics-IQ Verified is **58.2% ± 1.8** against a ceiling of 100%, and re-scoring six image-to-video models changed their ranking with a Kendall tau of only 0.46 ([Physics-IQ](https://github.com/google-deepmind/physics-IQ-benchmark); [arXiv 2606.18943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18943)). An LLM used directly as the simulator is weaker still: GPT-4 predicted **77.1%** of action-driven but at most **49.7%** of environment-driven state transitions in text games, humans scored 80% against a sampled LLM accuracy of 50%, and a 59.9% single-step accuracy compounds to under 1% over ten steps, with errors concentrated exactly where a faucet lives (temperature, apertures, electrical connections) ([Wang et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06485v1)). Even "composable" neural world models factorize one latent space rather than assembling independently authored modules; a November 2025 object-centric model "underperformed DreamerV3" on control because of representation shift during multi-object interactions ([arXiv 2511.06136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06136)).

The descriptive family, by contrast, is where a component library must plug in: an IFC 4.3 faucet is an `IfcValve` with `PredefinedType=FAUCET` or `MIXING` nested in an `IfcSanitaryTerminal`, and a pump is an `IfcPump` whose `Pset_PumpTypeCommon` carries flow-rate range, NPSH and nominal speed but no curve ([IfcValveTypeEnum](https://buildingsmart.or.kr/newsfile/ifc4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcValveTypeEnum.htm); [Pset_PumpTypeCommon](https://buildingsmart.or.kr/newsfile/ifc4_3/HTML/lexical/Pset_PumpTypeCommon.htm)). The pragmatic hybrid this research converges on is an explicit, typed, unit-bearing component skeleton, optionally wrapping a learned surrogate inside it, exported to a physics engine for spatial interaction and to a generative model only for rendering; no surveyed project delivers the first two layers.

## The physics already exists; the agent-facing packaging does not

### Open libraries and engines

Across fourteen research slices the same picture recurs: mature component models on one side, mature agent protocols on the other, and nothing in between that turns a component into something an agent can find and trust. On the modelling side, the Modelica Standard Library 4.1.0 (23 May 2025, BSD-3-Clause, ~560 issues and 392 PRs closed) ships `Modelica.Fluid.Machines.Pump`, `ControlledPump` and `PrescribedPump` with replaceable flow, power and efficiency characteristics and NPSH monitoring, and six valve models with `CvData` in {Av, Kv, Cv, OpPoint} ([Modelica Association](https://modelica.org/news/2025-05-23-release-msl-4_1_0/)).

Modelica's annotations standardize `Documentation`, `Dialog`, `unit`/`displayUnit`, `Icon`, `experiment` and `choices`, which makes it unusually machine-readable ([Modelica 3.6 spec](https://specification.modelica.org/maint/3.6/annotations.html)), and OpenModelica exposes `getClassNames`, `getComponents`, `getDocumentationAnnotation`, `addComponent`, `addConnection`, `setParameterValue`, `checkModel`, `simulate` and `buildModelFMU` over ZeroMQ ([OMC scripting API](https://openmodelica.org/doc/OpenModelicaUsersGuide/latest/scripting_api.html)). OpenModelica 1.27.0 (2 July 2026) added an experimental MCP server in OMEdit, tracked in issue #15385 as "the start, not the finished article", with a Linux-only prototype pending Qt6 on Windows; a third-party wrapper's issue states the server exposes 39 tools ([release](https://github.com/OpenModelica/OpenModelica/releases/tag/v1.27.0); [issue #15385](https://github.com/OpenModelica/OpenModelica/issues/15385); [modelica-wrapper #576](https://github.com/dicode-ayo/modelica-wrapper/issues/576)). OpenModelica is dual-licensed OSMC-PL 1.8 or AGPL v3, which matters for any hosted service ([OpenModelica repository, OSMC-License](https://github.com/OpenModelica/OpenModelica)).

### Vendor and community agent interfaces

The vendor world has gone further on the agent interface but not on the component. MathWorks ships a five-tool MATLAB MCP Server and a nine-tool Simulink Agentic Toolkit (`model_overview`, `model_read`, `model_edit`, `model_check`, `model_read_diagnostics`, `model_test`, `model_query_params`, `model_resolve_params`, `model_scan`) under a custom license restricted to MathWorks products ([matlab-mcp-server](https://github.com/matlab/matlab-mcp-server); [simulink-agentic-toolkit](https://github.com/matlab/simulink-agentic-toolkit)); in February 2026 Claude Code built a Simscape coffee-cooling model through that server, but only "after receiving specific skill documentation about Simscape best practices" and with manual layout fixes ([MathWorks blog](https://blogs.mathworks.com/simulink/2026/02/26/simulink-and-simscape-modeling-using-claude-code-and-the-matlab-mcp-server/)). Wolfram System Modeler 15 lets any MCP client "simulate, plot and analyze" Modelica models ([Wolfram](https://www.wolfram.com/system-modeler/what-is-new/index.php.en)).

Ansys' PyFluent-MCP (Apache-2.0, 20 tools in six groups, self-described as "deterministic infrastructure" that deliberately excludes agent loops) is one of the few production vendor MCP servers over a physics solver, though a CFD one ([PyFluent-MCP](https://github.com/ansys/pyfluent-mcp)). JuliaHub's Dyad is the closest product to the user's framing: an agent that "researches the physics", assembles components with potential-and-flow connectors, auto-generates unit tests and calibrates, sold at $0/$20/$60/$200 per month with the agent paid-only, backed by a **$65M Series B on 30 April 2026** ([Dyad agent blog](https://juliahub.com/blog/agentic-ai-dyad); [pricing](https://juliahub.com/pricing/dyad); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/juliahub-raises-65m-series-b-and-launches-dyad-3-0--bringing-agentic-ai-to-industrial-digital-twins-302758889.html)). But Dyad's compiler license and open-source status are not stated on its pages, no MCP, Python or JSON API for external agents is documented, and the agent post concedes that "correctness today is enforced primarily through tests" ([Dyad docs](https://help.juliahub.com/dyad/dev/)).

The most agent-oriented open Modelica tooling is a one-person project: MLQT exposes "more than 80 tools" and returns a class interface in under **600 tokens versus about 59,000** for the file, driving Dymola 2021+ or OpenModelica 1.24+ ([MLQT](https://github.com/mdempse1/MLQT)). The safety pattern exists in one small project too: @casys/mcp-modelica executes only approved kits with schema-bounded overrides and SHA-256 run ledgers and forbids arbitrary source ([JSR](https://jsr.io/@casys/mcp-modelica)). Two further Modelica MCP servers are minimal: Orthogonalpub's `modelica_simulation_mcp_server` exposes two tools over a cloud IDE, and dymola-mcp is a 12-tool stub needing a licensed Dymola ([Orthogonalpub](https://github.com/Orthogonalpub/modelica_simulation_mcp_server); [dymola-mcp](https://github.com/simonluke/dymola-mcp)). None of these Modelica MCP servers exposes a component catalogue; they expose the tool, not the parts.

### The exchange layer: FMI, SSP and their hosts

The exchange layer is standardized but empty. FMI 3.0.2 (27 November 2024) is the only widely deployed tool-neutral container for executable component models; its registry lists **289 tools, 108 of them for FMI 3.0, 161 exporting and 215 importing** ([FMI releases](https://github.com/modelica/fmi-standard/releases); [tools.json](https://fmi-standard.org/assets/tools.json)). SSP 2.0.1 (5 May 2026) describes composed systems of FMUs with parameter sets, and SSP-LS Traceability 1.0 (29 August 2025) adds SRMD keyword/value metadata; FMI-LS-REF 1.0.0-beta.1 (1 July 2026) adds a manifest linking an FMU to documentation, tests, sources and serialized states ([SSP releases](https://github.com/modelica/ssp-standard/releases); [SSP-LS Traceability](https://modelica.github.io/ssp-ls-traceability/main/); [FMI-LS-REF](https://modelica.github.io/fmi-ls-ref/main/)).

Yet no FMU registry or marketplace was found in this research: the only public corpora are eight Reference FMUs and a ~1,530-FMU cross-check archived in February 2022 ([Reference-FMUs](https://github.com/modelica/Reference-FMUs/releases); [fmi-cross-check](https://github.com/modelica/fmi-cross-check)). The Python consumer that matters is FMPy 0.3.32 (9 September 2026, BSD-2, FMI 1/2/3, **154,506 monthly downloads**); PyFMI is actively released but still FMI 1.0/2.0 only, and OMSimulator has had no stable release since November 2024 ([FMPy](https://pypi.org/project/FMPy/); [pypistats](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/fmpy/recent); [PyFMI releases](https://github.com/modelon-community/PyFMI/releases); [OMSimulator releases](https://github.com/OpenModelica/OMSimulator/releases)).

The only shipped agent artifact whose unit of exchange is an FMU is mcp-fmi (Novia, MIT, six tools on FMPy, 12 stars, last push 26 October 2025) ([mcp-fmi](https://github.com/Novia-RDI-Seafaring/mcp-fmi)); the strongest evidence that standards metadata plus an LLM can partially solve composition is a February 2026 OpenSCALING demonstrator by Saab, Parker Hannifin and eXXcellent in which an LLM used FMI/SSP/SSP-Traceability metadata through the easySSP MCP server for auditable integration calls, a news item rather than a paper ([ITEA](https://itea4.org/news/llm-assisted-fmi-ssp-component-integration-in-aircraft-system-simulators.html)).

## Agents fail at writing physics and succeed at composing it

### Generating Modelica

The single most decision-relevant finding is quantitative and repeated: LLMs write syntactically plausible physical models that do not simulate correctly, and the failure disappears when the agent is confined to selecting, parameterizing and wiring verified components under a compiler or simulator loop. ModiGen benchmarked 126 Modelica components with four-stage OpenModelica verification and found baseline pass@1 of 95.56% load, 50.16% consistency, 27.38% simulation and **24.44% functional** for GPT-4o, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 23.57% functional; fine-tuning plus graph-RAG plus feedback lifted GPT-4o to 36.90%, and the named failure classes were syntactic violations, "semantic hallucinations, where LLMs fabricate non-existent library components or parameters" and "physical inconsistencies" ([ModiGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18460)).

The July 2026 fluid-systems benchmark of ten LLMs and six prompting strategies translating a neutral graph into WNTR and MSL code found Modelica syntactic pass@1 as high as 0.87 (Kimi-K2.5) yet simulation fidelity of **0.0 ± 0.0**, with the paper stating that even for the coding-agent configuration "simulation fidelity remains at zero across all attempts" ([arXiv 2607.29389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29389)). PNNL's Buildings CDL study found GPT-4o produced no executable code zero-shot, Claude Sonnet 4 reached 100% on basic blocks and 83% on control modules with engineered prompts, failures needed one to eight hours of human repair, RAG retrieved "Or" when "And" was wanted so deterministic name search beat it, and "current LLMs cannot assess simulation results or validate behavioral correctness" ([arXiv 2509.14623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14623)).

The Modelica Agent Workflow Benchmark (August 2026) shows the residual problem even with a purpose-built harness: Pufibara solved 202 of 232 tasks versus 185–187 for Claude Code, but on 38 hard generation tasks Claude Code submitted **21 executable-but-contract-violating** models against Pufibara's 4, with failure modes of physical inconsistency despite compiling, specification violations, requirement drift and stale evidence ([arXiv 2608.23653](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23653)).

### Adjacent formalisms

The same pattern holds in every adjacent formalism, which is why the report treats it as structural rather than Modelica-specific. On Planetarium, **96.1%** of GPT-4o's PDDL problems parse and 94.4% solve, but only **24.8%** are semantically correct ([arXiv 2407.03321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03321)); on Text2World the best domain-generation model reaches 72.3% executability (89.1% after three correction rounds) yet precondition/effect F1 stays below 60% ([arXiv 2502.13092](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13092)). SimLLM's Qwen2.5-Coder-7B produced executable SimPy 80.4% of the time before fine-tuning while its instruction-mechanism consistency was **0%**, rising to 76.8% only after training on 7,200 validated pairs ([arXiv 2601.06543](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06543v1)). SysML v2 generation is worse: the best BLEU on SysMBench is 4% and the best SysMEval-F1 62% across 17 LLMs ([arXiv 2508.03215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03215)).

Physics reasoning itself sits at a 40–60% ceiling: DeepSeek-R1 scores 56.75% on PhysReason, o1-mini 49.78% on UGPhysics against more than 90% on MATH, o3-mini 59.9% on PHYSICS, and on the interactive PhysMent benchmark budget-averaged accuracy runs 28.6–50.0% with the single best result at 66.7% ([PhysReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12054v1); [UGPhysics](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00334v2); [PhysMent](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13152)). SciCode-Verified is a caution against over-reading any single number: an audit found 263 defects in the benchmark itself, and correcting them moved main-problem accuracy for 12 frontier snapshots from 9–27% to 69–92% ([arXiv 2608.04975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04975)).

### Composing over typed tools

When the agent is instead given a small, typed tool surface over an existing simulator, success rates jump and stay high. EPANET-Agentic nests TaskExecutor, CodeRunner and DataAnalyzer agents as function-call tools over WNTR and reports "100% success rate and tool invocation accuracy with no human interventions" on L-Town, C-Town and Net3 ([Water Research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135426001156)), whereas the earlier LLM-EPANET framework that generated free-form EPyT code achieved 56–81% overall on 69 queries ([arXiv 2503.16191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16191)). Foam-Agent 2.0 exposes OpenFOAM through MCP with a reviewer loop of up to 25 repair iterations and reports 88.2% on 110 tasks with Claude 3.5 Sonnet against 55.5% for the prior baseline ([arXiv 2509.18178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18178)).

The wastewater study is the sharpest demonstration that agents should query models rather than memorize them: grounding an LLM in a live simulator oracle gave **99.5%** on 198 causal questions versus 79% for parameter injection and 75.8% for a learned retriever ([arXiv 2608.05151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05151)). The Scenic result explains the mechanism: according to the paper's HTML version as surfaced in a search summary (not re-verified against the primary page), directly prompting an LLM to write Scenic often yields code that does not compile, whereas a constrained intermediate representation reached 100% on environment and road attributes and 97–98% on oracle and actor trajectories ([arXiv 2602.20644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20644)). The lesson for a component library is that its highest-value artifact is not new physics but a compact, typed catalogue with deterministic lookup, worked examples and a verification loop.

## A bathroom faucet can be assembled today but not discovered, coupled or reused

### Behaviour models

For the interior-design or BIM agent, every needed ingredient exists somewhere, and none of the places where a 3D scene lives can host it. The behaviour itself is a solved modelling problem: Buildings' `TwoWayEqualPercentage` or `TwoWayTable` gives flow versus opening from a Kv with leakage `l` default 0.0001 and `strokeTime` default 120 s, `ThreeWayEqualPercentageLinear` gives hot/cold mixing with `fraK`, and `Buildings.DHC.Loads.HotWater.ThermostaticMixingValve` is semantically the user's faucet, drawing `yMixSet * mMix_flow_nominal` at `TMixSet` from `port_hot` and `port_col` under a PI controller with `k=1`, `Ti=0.5 s`, `Td=0.1 s`, consuming the mixed water in an internal sink and asserting if the setpoint is missed ([Valves](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_Actuators_Valves.html); [HotWater](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_DHC_Loads_HotWater.html)).

EnergyPlus' `WaterUse:Equipment` supplies the same semantics in a whole-building tool, attempting "to meet a scheduled target temperature at the tap by mixing hot and cold water flows" with an unmet-temperature failure mode when hot supply is short, but it is schedule-driven with no handle state and no supply-pressure dependence ([EnergyPlus I/O](https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/26-1/input-output-reference/group-water-systems.html)). A pure-Python faucet is roughly 50–100 lines on top of `fluids.control_valve` (Cv/Kv/Av per IEC 60534-8-4 and 8-3) plus a CoolProp enthalpy balance, but `fluids` deliberately ships no stateful faucet object ([fluids.control_valve](https://fluids.readthedocs.io/fluids.control_valve.html)).

The cost of running such a model is negligible: OpenModelica's nightly regression translates `TwoWayValves` (145 equations) in about 0.10 s and simulates 2,500 Euler steps in about 9 ms, and the 1,350-equation DHW example containing a `ThermostaticMixingValve` translates in 1.3 s and simulates two days in 47 ms with DASSL, so a compiled faucet costs microseconds per step and a Unity `FixedUpdate` could afford hundreds of them, while the edit-recompile loop is 6–9 s wall-clock ([TwoWayValves.sim](https://libraries.openmodelica.org/branches/master/Buildings_latest/files/Buildings_latest_Buildings.Fluid.Actuators.Valves.Examples.TwoWayValves.sim); [StorageTank.sim](https://libraries.openmodelica.org/branches/master/Buildings_latest/files/Buildings_latest_Buildings.DHC.Loads.HotWater.Examples.StorageTankWithExternalHeatExchanger.sim)).

### Integration path through FMI

The integration path into a scene runs through FMI and is thinner than it looks. `Buildings.Fluid.FMI.ExportContainers.ReplaceableTwoPort` wraps any `PartialTwoPort`, so a two-way faucet valve FMU is one redeclaration away, with inputs `m_flow`, `p`, `T` and opening `y` and outputs the outlet state and pressure drop; but the container is causal with mass flow as an input, so a "turn the handle and flow appears from supply pressure" faucet must be authored with `use_p_in=true` and the host closing the pressure-flow loop or an embedded `Source_T`/`Sink_T` pair, the mixing valve needs a bespoke container with two `Adaptors.Inlet` blocks and no outlet, three-way valves do not fit the two-port container at all, and none of the 14 shipped example FMUs is a valve ([Buildings.Fluid.FMI](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_FMI_UsersGuide.html); [Examples.FMUs](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_FMI_ExportContainers_Examples_FMUs.html)).

On the host side, Dassault's Unity-FMI-Addon supports "only a limited subset of FMI 2.0 for co-simulation" and has had no code change since 19 June 2018 despite a 2026 push date, ORNL's Unreal plugin implements FMI 1.0/2.0 with floats and booleans only and a last commit of 15 August 2025, Godot has zero repositories, and Omniverse has no official extension, only a July 2026 one-person scaffold that "has not yet run inside Kit" and a forum thread whose "experimental" reply is not verifiably from NVIDIA staff ([Unity-FMI-Addon](https://github.com/CATIA-Systems/Unity-FMI-Addon); [UEFMI](https://github.com/ORNL-Modelica/UnrealEngine-FMIPlugin); [omni.fmi](https://github.com/RGoharimehr/omni.fmi); [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/fmi-fmu-in-nvidia-omniverse/360460)). The browser path requires Emscripten-compiling a source-code FMI 2.0 CS FMU via Bodylight.js (GPL-3.0, 0.5–2 MB output), and FMI-LS-WASM has Rust and C hosts but no JavaScript host ([Bodylight.js-FMU-Compiler](https://github.com/creative-connections/Bodylight.js-FMU-Compiler); [fmi-ls-wasm](https://github.com/modelica/fmi-ls-wasm)).

### Scene formats and engines

The scene formats themselves have no slot for the model. UsdPhysics is "a baseline initial extension to USD that enables the minimum set of common concepts required to represent rigid body physics", OpenExec (preview since OpenUSD v25.08) is "a value-driven computation system, as opposed to being event-driven" whose proposal never mentions simulation, and NVIDIA's 18 March 2025 blog stated an intent for "co-standardization and conceptual data mapping between OpenUSD and Functional Mock-up Interface (FMI) for co-simulation" that has produced no proposal, schema or AOUSD forum thread since ([UsdPhysics](https://openusd.org/release/api/usd_physics_page_front.html); [OpenExec proposal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PixarAnimationStudios/OpenUSD-proposals/main/proposals/openexec/README.md); [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/using-openusd-for-modular-and-scalable-robotic-simulation-and-development/)). SimReady "currently covers basic static props (ones that don't move or articulate)" ([SimReady spec](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/simready/latest/overview/simready-spec.html)).

glTF's KHR_interactivity is the one asset format that can carry behaviour: it is listed as ratified in the Khronos registry and implemented in Babylon.js with all 13 Khronos samples passing, though the only press release (16 July 2026) says "submitted for ratification"; a lumped faucet is expressible as custom variables for handle angle, hot fraction and supply pressure with math nodes on `event/onTick`, but with no units, no ports, no solver and no way to couple to a room-level supply except custom events ([glTF registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/main/extensions/README.md); [Khronos press](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/gltf-interactivity-extension-submitted-for-ratification)).

Embodied simulators encode a faucet as a state predicate: in AI2-THOR it is one `Toggleable` bit, in OmniGibson water emission belongs to the `sink.n.01` synset (31 models, 27 ready, 281 tasks) while `faucet.n.01` has no toggleable or particle-source ability at all, and `ParticleSource` conditions act as Boolean gates, so a `FUNCTION` condition can switch emission but not modulate its rate ([AI2-THOR](https://ai2thor.allenai.org/ithor/documentation/objects/object-types/); [sink.n.01](https://behavior.stanford.edu/knowledgebase/synsets/sink.n.01.html); [faucet.n.01](https://behavior.stanford.edu/knowledgebase/synsets/faucet.n.01.html); [particle_source_or_sink.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StanfordVL/BEHAVIOR-1K/main/OmniGibson/omnigibson/object_states/particle_source_or_sink.py)). Infinigen v1.19.0 generates articulated faucets with joint metadata and physics parameters and exports USD/URDF, which is the geometry-and-kinematics half of the component, with no hydraulics ([Infinigen v1.19.0](https://api.github.com/repos/princeton-vl/infinigen/releases/tags/v1.19.0)).

Two engines offer more than a predicate. MuJoCo's engine-plugin API (actuator, sensor, passive-force and SDF plugins with serialized state, declared on MJCF elements and composable via `attach`) is the closest thing to a pluggable component with internal dynamics in any engine, so a faucet or pump could be written as a plugin, but the ABI is C, the semantics are mechanical (forces on joints) and there is no registry or metadata for an agent to discover such a component ([MuJoCo extension API](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/programming/extension.html)); Genesis (Apache-2.0, v1.4.1, 12 September 2026) is the one broad engine that puts SPH/PBD liquids and rigid bodies behind a single Python API with URDF/MJCF/USD import, but it has no faucet component, so an agent scripts an emitter ([Genesis](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis)). The MCP servers an agent would use to build the room (blender-mcp at 29.2k stars, Unity MCP, Unreal MCP) expose "execute arbitrary code", so any faucet behaviour an agent writes there is bespoke and undiscoverable by the next agent ([blender-mcp](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)).

### Data gaps

Two data gaps sit underneath the tooling gaps. The MEP tier that BIM tools actually implement is a scalar: Revit converts fixture units to flow with the 2006 IPC Table E103.3(3) and sizes pipes by Darcy–Weisbach with Haaland or Colebrook friction, MagiCAD implements fourteen national sizing standards from DIN 1988-300 to BS 8558, and the Revit 2027 MCP is read-only with seven generic tools, so a scene-pluggable faucet would need loading-unit values under several code systems rather than one physical curve ([Revit pipe sizing](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2025/ENU/Revit-MEPEng/files/GUID-E1121308-E1E5-4AF1-B2FA-913CBD35F656.htm); [MagiCAD standards](https://www.magicad.com/mep-design/resources/standards-and-localisation/); [Autodesk MCP hub](https://help.autodesk.com/view/ADSKMCP/ENU/)).

And a real product's Kv is not retrievable programmatically: the IFC slot `Pset_ValveTypeCommon.FlowCoefficient` exists and bSDD answers definition queries, but bSDD "stores only definitions", MagiCAD Cloud (1,000,000 objects, 300+ manufacturers), NBS Source (28,000+ objects) and BIMobject expose no documented API, and the tap-and-mixer data model that does carry behaviour, VDI 3805 Blatt 17 for drinking-water fittings, is a paid standard with no open reader ([bSDD](https://github.com/buildingSMART/bSDD); [MagiCAD Cloud](https://www.magicad.com/en/magicad-cloud/); [VDI 3805](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/VDI_3805)).

The nearest free source of real tap and mixer product data is the BDH-run bim4hvac portal, which distributes manufacturer catalogues in VDI 3805 / EN ISO 16757 format, including a drinking-water-fittings (Trinkwasserarmaturen) category among its thirteen, stated to be free of charge for all trades and exportable as Revit, IFC, DWG, DXF and STEP, with a "Web-Service" download entry but no documented API; the format specification itself remains paid, so an agent still cannot parse what it downloads ([bim4hvac](https://www.bim4hvac.com/)).

Interior-design tools have no attachment point at all: Roomle's documentation index has no page on physics or plumbing and Homestyler's "Spark" agent does layout and rendering ([Roomle llms.txt](https://docs.roomle.com/rubens/llms.txt); [Homestyler](https://www.homestyler.com/)). Commercially, no interior-design or BIM pilot with behavioural fixtures was found, and the nearest AEC automation vendor, Augmenta, treats building design as "a geometry problem, not a language problem" with plumbing "coming soon" ([Augmenta](https://www.augmenta.ai/)).

### What is missing for the faucet

What is missing for the faucet, in order of severity, is therefore a port-to-scene binding schema, a maintained FMI 3.0 or WASM host inside any scene engine (or a MuJoCo-plugin equivalent with discovery metadata), a small faucet manifest (Kv or flow-pressure curve, aerator limit, mixing law, minimum supply pressure, loading units) mapped to IFC and VDI 3805, and a source of real product parameters, for which bim4hvac's VDI 3805 catalogues are the nearest candidate once an open reader exists.

## A purification-plant pump has four open hosts and no open manufacturer data

### The four open hosts

The pump example is better served, which is why it is the better first market. Four open hosts already model a pump for a plant-analysis agent, each with a different abstraction. WNTR represents a `HeadPump` by a curve it fits to H = A − B·Q^C (one point gives A = 4/3 H, B = 1/3 H/Q², C = 2; two points give a line; more points go to scipy `curve_fit`), a `PowerPump` by constant power, supports speed patterns and a declarative `Control`/`Rule` language with seven priorities, and serializes the whole network to a dictionary or JSON in SI units, though without schema validation ([WNTR HeadPump](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/apidoc/wntr.network.elements.HeadPump.html); [WNTR model I/O](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/model_io.html)). EPANET 2.3 (released 18 July 2025; latest patch 2.3.5, 20 February 2026; MIT) added positional control valves and FAVAD leakage that WNTR, which keeps EPANET 2.2 semantics, reaches only through owa-epanet, EPyT or the WASM port epanet-js 0.9.0 ([EPANET releases](https://github.com/OpenWaterAnalytics/EPANET/releases.atom); [epanet-js](https://github.com/epanet-js/epanet-js-toolkit)).

WaterTAP's `Pump` inherits the IDAES `PressureChanger`, is single-phase, steady-state and isothermal, offers constant, flow-dependent (Kuritza et al. 2017, "not validated for high pressure applications") and flow-head-dependent efficiency, optional user-written performance-curve constraints and at least two degrees of freedom, and sits inside a catalogue of RO 0D (four DOF minimum), UV-AOP and 115 zero-order units whose YAML defaults are "generally only meant to help initiate model usage for new users" ([WaterTAP Pump](https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/technical_reference/unit_models/pump.html); [zero-order units](https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/technical_reference/unit_models/zero_order_unit_models/index.html)). Its `pump.yaml`, sometimes cited as performance data, is a costing file: 0.051 kWh/m³ energy intensity and a capital-cost power law (a = 40299 USD_2007, b = 0.8657), with no curve ([pump.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/watertap-org/watertap/main/watertap/data/techno_economic/pump.yaml)).

Modelica Buildings' `SpeedControlled_y` computes pressure rise from user-provided operating points with affinity laws for reduced speed and the Euler-number efficiency method by default, and it is the only open library where a pump instantiates from a real manufacturer record ([Movers UsersGuide](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_Movers_UsersGuide.html)). The fourth host is pandapipes 0.14.0 (26 May 2026, BSD, Fraunhofer IEE / University of Kassel), which models a pump as a standard type fitted by `np.polyfit` from curve points via `PumpStdType.from_list` and solved with `pipeflow` ([pandapipes](https://pypi.org/project/pandapipes/)); EnergyPlus' `Pump:VariableSpeed` (default design head 179,352 Pa, cubic part-load curve) and DWSIM 10's pump unit operation are further alternatives, the first schedule-bound to a building loop and the second inside a GPL process flowsheet ([EnergyPlus I/O](https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/26-1/input-output-reference/group-water-systems.html); [DWSIM 10](https://github.com/DanWBR/dwsim10)).

### Parameterizing four hosts from one record

The `Stratos25slash1to6` record, digitized from Wilo's product finder with WebPlotDigitizer, holds **8 flow points per curve** from 8.46e-06 to 0.00187 m³/s with pressure from 34,808 to 13,042 Pa and power from 27.79 to 75.18 W, revision history back to 2014, a note that "the pump curve saturation at elevated speeds is not enforced", and no datasheet revision, test grade or data license; the 3.55 m digitized shut-off head for a nominal "1–6 m" pump suggests one speed setting, an ambiguity this research did not resolve ([Stratos25slash1to6.mo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lbl-srg/modelica-buildings/master/Buildings/Fluid/Movers/Data/Pumps/Wilo/Stratos25slash1to6.mo)).

The same record parameterizes all four hosts in a few lines, and the results will diverge because each transforms the data differently: WNTR refits eight points to a three-parameter curve, pandapipes fits a polynomial of chosen degree and treats reverse flow as zero lift while `to_json` drops custom standard types, Modelica interpolates the raw arrays, and WaterTAP needs a user-written head constraint inside the performance-curve block with scaling to targets of 0.01–100 before IPOPT will converge; only WNTR emits a host-independent JSON description ([pandapipes std_type_class.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/e2nIEE/pandapipes/develop/src/pandapipes/std_types/std_type_class.py); [WaterTAP scaling](https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/how_to_guides/how_to_scale_a_model.html)). None of the four documents a single-pump solve time.

### Discovery through IFC, OPC UA and AAS

On the descriptive side an agent can discover a pump's class and ratings today from IFC `Pset_PumpTypeCommon` (FlowRateRange, NPSH, NominalRotationSpeed), OPC 40223's 51 object types, SAREF4WATR and Matter's Pump Configuration and Control cluster, but three of nine bim2sim pump attributes and two of six valve attributes have no Pset source and no default ([hvac_elements.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BIM2SIM/bim2sim/development/bim2sim/elements/hvac_elements.py)), and, judging from the fact that bim2sim needs a Decisions mechanism because of the "mixed quality of IFC-files" and ifctrano works from geometry alone because space boundaries "are rarely available in IFC files", real IFC exports cannot be relied on to populate these sets, which is why every IFC-to-Modelica generator falls back to defaults, interactive decisions or VDI 3805 product mapping; no source quantifies how often the pump and valve Psets are actually filled ([bim2sim big-picture](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BIM2SIM/bim2sim/development/docs/source/big-picture.md); [ifctrano](https://github.com/andoludo/ifctrano)).

The AAS route is schematically complete: IDTA 02005 v1.1 (committed 25 March 2026) carries ports, variables with units and causality, flow and stream prefixes and an FMI 3.0 binary connector, cites SSP for "create and maintain a simulation of systems", and research prototypes (Göllner et al. 2021 with FMI; Bu & Lee 2025) have generated models from AAS, but both IDTA versions still describe automatic integration as a future step and no manufacturer-populated registry was found ([IDTA 02005 folders](https://api.github.com/repos/admin-shell-io/submodel-templates/contents/published/Provision%20of%20Simulation%20Models/1); [Göllner et al.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/ieem50564.2021.9673089)).

### Agent-side integration

The agent-side integration pattern is already proven in this domain. EPANET-Agentic's 100% result, EnergyPlus-MCP's 35 tools including `discover_hvac_loops` and `get_loop_topology`, and DWSIM 10's 27-tool MCP server over flowsheets, streams, unit operations and solver all show the shape: a deterministic tool layer over an existing simulator with an orchestrator LLM ([EnergyPlus-MCP](https://github.com/LBNL-ETA/EnergyPlus-MCP); [DWSIM 10](https://github.com/DanWBR/dwsim10)). Three further signals sit in the same domain: Autodesk lists an "InfoWorks Hydraulic Modeling MCP (coming soon)" for InfoWorks ICM and WS Pro, the first vendor MCP announced over a hydraulic solver ([Autodesk APS blog](https://aps.autodesk.com/blog/building-agentic-ai-whats-new-autodesk-platform-services)); WaterAdmin (April 2026) puts an LLM context tier over an optimizer that schedules pumps and valves in EPANET ([arXiv 2604.10343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10343)); and LBNL's Flowsheet Inspector (8 September 2026, CC-BY-4.0), a VS Code extension built to verify that AI-generated IDAES/WaterTAP/PrOMMiS flowsheet code "matches what the user intended, as well as verifying the results themselves", is a validation-need signal in exactly the purification domain ([Zenodo 22663704](https://zenodo.org/records/22663704)).

No MCP server for EPANET, WNTR or WaterTAP was found in this research, though the absence could not be proven because the search budget expired before a targeted registry scan; Modelica MCP servers do exist (the OMEdit prototype, MLQT, @casys/mcp-modelica, Orthogonalpub, dymola-mcp), but none exposes a component catalogue. The commercial wastewater simulators are moving the same way from behind their licences: GPS-X MaaS runs "mechanistic models in the cloud and makes them available on demand through secure APIs", SUMO supports twins "in any kind of software environment (C/C++, Python, .NET on Windows or Linux)" with an OPC-UA link to Emerson Ovation, and ifak's SIMBA#water 7.5 adds inline neural networks and a SCADA library, but their models are closed and no LLM interface is documented ([GPS-X](https://www.hydromantis.com/GPSX.html); [SUMO](https://dynamita.com/services.html); [SIMBA#](https://www.ifak.eu/en/products/simba-water/)).

### Manufacturer data

What is missing for the pump is manufacturer data with provenance first, then packaging. Open curve-bearing corpora amount to the 18 Wilo records, three generic pandapipes curves, ASHRAE 205 example maps for fans, motors and drives (no pump representation exists among RS0001–RS0007) and EPANET example networks; the large corpora (VDI 3805 catalogues, Wilo-Select, Grundfos Product Center, Xylem) sit behind logins or paid standards, HI Energy Rating publishes PEI/CEI ratings without curves or bulk export, and the Grundfos developer host does not resolve ([ASHRAE 205 schema](https://data.ashrae.org/standard205/schema.html); [HI ER](https://er.pumps.org/); [bim4hvac](https://www.bim4hvac.com/)).

The closest existing agent-facing pump-data artifact is the `pump-performance-db` agent skill (updated 20 September 2026, installable into Claude Code, Cursor, Windsurf and Copilot), which "Provides access to pump curves and specifications from major manufacturers" but discloses neither which manufacturers, what format, what licence, nor whether the data is real or synthetic; it is a demand signal that also illustrates the provenance problem, and the commercial PumpCurves directory's claimed 8,400+ curves from 50+ manufacturers come with no machine-readable format ([pump-performance-db](https://agentskill.sh/@andrewthomastaylor/pump-performance-db); [PumpCurves](https://pumpprosdirectory.com/)). EU Regulation 547/2012 legally obliges manufacturers to publish MEI, BEP/part-load/overload efficiencies and "pump performance curves including efficiency characteristics" on free-access websites, which makes a licence-cleared, provenance-rich "EU 547/2012 curve corpus" the most plausible first data project, though it requires digitization exactly as LBNL did ([EU 547/2012 Annex II](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/547/annex/II)).

## Demand is real at the two ends and unproven in the middle

### Who pays today

Would such a library be useful, and to whom? The evidence says yes for the plant-analysis constituency, plausibly for MEP and data-centre automation, weakly for smart home, and not yet demonstrably for the bathroom-view designer. Water utilities and their consultants already pay for model-based tooling: Transcend's generative design lists WSP, Wessex Water, Xylem, HDR, Scottish Water, Severn Trent and Sabesp as customers with one citing up to 80% preliminary-design cost reduction, Hydromantis runs Mantis.AI on GPS-X process models, and Binnies built a pump-fault twin on JuliaHub predicting faults with more than 90% accuracy from four sensor inputs ([Transcend](https://transcendinfra.com/); [Mantis.AI](https://www.hydromantis.com/MantisAI.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/juliahub-raises-65m-series-b-and-launches-dyad-3-0--bringing-agentic-ai-to-industrial-digital-twins-302758889.html)). WNTR sees 18,083 monthly PyPI downloads and pandapower 341,257, an open substrate agents can already write against ([pypistats WNTR](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/wntr/recent); [pypistats pandapower](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/pandapower/recent)). In buildings, the PNNL result of cutting module development from 10–20 hours to 4–6 hours is the only measured productivity gain, and it came from library grounding rather than generation ([arXiv 2509.14623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14623)).

Industrial operations platforms have the agent loops and connectors but not the physics: XMPro reports 3,400+ active control loops and 150+ OT/IT connectors with water utilities among 11 industries, AVEVA announced an industrial knowledge graph and planned MCP integrations with the CONNECT release due in Q1 2027, and Cognite Atlas AI and C3 operate on time series, graphs and documents with no physics or behavioural models mentioned, which is exactly the slot beneath the graph that a behavioural component library could fill ([XMPro](https://xmpro.com/); [AVEVA](https://www.aveva.com/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/aveva-announces-new-capabilities-to-embed-ai-across-industrial-organizations-and-data-infrastructure-at-aveva-world-2026/); [Cognite](https://www.cognite.com/en/resources/blog/cognite-atlas-ai-september-product-release-accelerating-agentic-ai-at-scale)).

### Indirect signals and where capital flows

The demand signals for agent-accessible physical models are indirect but consistent. Home Assistant's MCP server reached **4.3% of active installations** within about eighteen months of MCP's release, the OPC Foundation now distributes its pump specification as "Markdown (AI Friendly, Low Token Count)" and "RAG Chunks (JSONL for Ollama / local models)", JuliaHub used LLM agents to translate the Modelica Standard Library into Dyad, and the 2025–2026 arXiv burst on LLM agents for Modelica, Simulink, fluid-system and digital-twin modelling spans eight separate groups (Text2Model, ModiGen, PNNL's Buildings CDL work, SimuAgent, the fluid-system benchmark, and three survey, simulation-agent and digital-twin-agent papers), five of them producing or benchmarking Modelica, Simulink or fluid-system code directly ([HA MCP](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/mcp_server/); [OPC 40223](https://reference.opcfoundation.org/Pumps/v100/docs/); [DyadLang](https://github.com/DyadLang); [arXiv 2503.02167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02167); [arXiv 2505.13761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13761); [arXiv 2606.20761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20761)).

Capital, however, flows to the two ends of the spectrum: PhysicsX raised a **$300M Series C at about $2.4B** (8 June 2026), Neural Concept $100M (18 December 2025), Luminary $72M (15 September 2025) for physics surrogates, and Dealroom's secondary tally puts 2026 world-model funding at $3.2B, while the only funded company selling an agent that composes reusable acausal component libraries is JuliaHub ([PhysicsX](https://www.physicsx.ai/newsroom/physicsx-announces-300m-series-c-to-accelerate-physics-ai-for-industrial-engineering); [Neural Concept](https://www.neuralconcept.com/post/neural-concept-closes-100m-funding-round-led-by-growth-equity-at-goldman-sachs-alternatives-to-scale-ai-native-engineering); [Luminary](https://luminary.ai/resources/luminary-cloud-secures-72m-series-b-to-lead-the-physics-ai-era/)). Luminary is also the only physics-AI vendor found with a documented MCP server, and Collimator, a cloud-Simulink-with-AI startup, appears to have died: its PyPI package was archived on 8 April 2025 and both domains fail DNS, a caution that distribution matters more than modelling novelty ([Luminary docs](https://docs.luminary.ai/); [pycollimator](https://pypi.org/project/pycollimator/)).

The paid market for reusable physical component models is real but tool-locked: the Modelica Association lists **59 commercial libraries from 16 vendors**, Modelon sells a 17-library suite through named-user Impact licences, and none has a storefront, per-component pricing or machine-readable metadata ([Modelica libraries](https://github.com/modelica/www.modelica.org/blob/main/content/libraries.md); [Modelon](https://www.modelon.com/modelon-library-suite-modelica-libraries/)). The one genuine marketplace of behavioural component models, ISG's TwinStore, is a certified catalogue of "4D" models for one virtual-commissioning tool, covering conveyors, grippers, robots, sensors and drives, with no process equipment, no pricing and no API ([TwinStore](https://start.twinstore.de/)). The correct claim is therefore that no open, tool-neutral, API-accessible marketplace of behavioural component models was found, not that none exists.

### What the adoption literature says

The adoption literature both supports and qualifies the thesis. A 2026 survey of 143 professionals found equipment cost and training time as top barriers and a stark awareness-to-use gap, **60% familiar with digital twins but only 11% working with them**, with the gap "not based on a lack of technical skills but more on the lack of their integration at a system level" ([FSC-CCF](https://fsc-ccf.ca/research/technology-adoption/)). A pre-validated, agent-composable library attacks training time, skills and modelling cost directly, but it does not solve system-level integration unless it ships with SCADA, BIM and home-automation connectors and a calibration story, and two decades of Modelica commercial libraries without mass adoption suggest usability and distribution, not component availability, are the binding constraints.

The September 2026 review of 66 LLM-for-HVAC studies found "Only four studies reach pilot-level evidence, and none reports sustained operational deployment", with LLMs recommended as "semantic and workflow layers rather than autonomous HVAC controllers" ([arXiv 2609.05314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05314)). The use cases with the clearest value are therefore offline, advisory and diagnostic: plant what-if analysis, fault diagnosis, design-time sizing and commissioning checks for pumps, valves and tanks, and a plant-behaviour oracle for LLM-generated control code, which the entire LLM4PLC lineage lacks (its verifiers are compilers, model checkers or live PLCs, never a model of the equipment being controlled) ([SemaPLC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18565)). The bathroom faucet is a fine demonstration vehicle and a weak business; the interior-design market has no behavioural fixture pilots, no monetized agent surface and a geometry-first competitor set.

## Six unsolved problems separate a demo from a dependable library

### 1. Composition semantics

The first problem is composition semantics. Within one acausal tool, composing well-formed continuous components is solved by index reduction and structural balancing, but initialization, structural singularity and mode switching still need experts: Modelon's guidance defines a structurally singular model as one where "the number of variables is not equal to the number of equations" and diagnoses it by local balancing counts, Olsson and Henningsson found that fluid steady-state initialization "results depend significantly on the chosen start values" and needs homotopy, and Benveniste et al. (December 2025) show multimode DAE mode changes can produce impulses and under-specified restarts that users "manually smooth out" ([Modelon](https://help.modelon.com/latest/trouble_shooting/structurally_singular_system/); [Olsson & Henningsson](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/modelica/article/download/187/147/90); [arXiv 2512.10580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10580)).

The everyday faucet and pump regimes (valve closed, pump off, zero flow) are precisely the singular ones, which is why Buildings recommends `use_riseTime=true` with 30 s and why an agent needs a typed pre-flight (`checkModel`-style balance, connector and unit checks) before any solver run. Across tools, FMI deliberately leaves cross-FMU algebraic loops, step-size control and rollback to the importer, industrial platforms "rarely produce rollback-capable systems", and the co-simulation survey still names "generic approaches for modular, stable and accurate coupling" as the field's main need ([FMI 3.0.2](https://fmi-standard.org/docs/3.0.2/); [COSTARICA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11752); [Gomes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00686)).

FMUs are causal black boxes, so acausal "connect two pipes" reasoning must happen before export or in an ontology layer, and only OSP-IS, a maritime ontology, gives FMI variables semantic meaning ([OSP specification](https://opensimulationplatform.com/specification/)). The practical consequence for the recommendations below is that a pump-plus-valve-plus-pipe loop should be composed at the Modelica level and compiled as one unit, not wired together from separately compiled FMUs unless a co-simulation master that handles the resulting cross-FMU loop is part of the design.

### 2. Units and provenance

The second problem is units and provenance, which nothing checks. FMI 3.0.2 defines units but does not require importers to check them, QUDT (CC BY 4.0, updated monthly) has dimension vectors for "dimensional analysis of equations" yet no tool found wires it into FMI or Modelica checking, MSL 4.0.0 renamed `Modelica.SIunits` to `Modelica.Units.SI` and broke compatibility, and TPS-CalcBench's error taxonomy includes unit errors and fabricated formulas among LLM outputs that are "numerically reasonable but physically invalid" ([QUDT](https://qudt.org/); [MSL 4.0.0](https://github.com/modelica/ModelicaStandardLibrary/releases/tag/v4.0.0); [TPS-CalcBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17966)). FMI makes no determinism or reproducibility guarantee, state get/set/serialize are optional capabilities, and SSP-LS Traceability is still "actively being worked on", so an agent re-running a composed model on another host may get different numbers with no standard record of why ([ssp-ls-traceability](https://github.com/modelica/ssp-ls-traceability)).

### 3. Validation

The third problem is validation: compile-and-simulate is an insufficient oracle. The MAWB benchmark's executable-but-contract-violating submissions, PNNL's finding that LLMs cannot assess simulation results, and JutulGPT's admission that "choices resolved tacitly through simulator defaults are invisible to the assumption log" all point to the need for machine-checkable behavioural contracts per component (conservation, monotonicity of flow versus pressure, response-time bounds), which STL monitoring with rtamt could supply as an acceptance-test layer but which no library ships ([JutulGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00214); [rtamt](https://github.com/nickovic/rtamt)).

### 4. Credibility and fidelity choice

The fourth problem is credibility and fidelity choice. ASME V&V 40 scales required evidence with model influence and decision consequence and its authors state the judgement "cannot be algorithmic"; the FDA's November 2023 guidance covers first-principles models "and not statistical or data-driven (e.g., machine learning or artificial intelligence-based) models", so an LLM-generated Modelica model is in scope in principle while the human artifacts it expects (context of use, adequacy assessment, credibility report) exist only if the agent workflow produces them; NIST says the ISO 23247 digital-twin framework "requires extension with credibility considerations" ([Morrison et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493688/); [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/assessing-credibility-computational-modeling-and-simulation-medical-device-submissions); [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/credibility-consideration-digital-twins-manufacturing)). Fidelity selection is solved only inside multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization loops with a cost model; no source shows an agent choosing lumped versus distributed physics for a component, and a library can only encode fidelity as a discrete attribute with a validity domain and cost ([Do & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13050)).

### 5. Calibration and identifiability

The fifth problem is calibration and identifiability for the plant case: sequential Bayesian updating of twins is formalized, StructuralIdentifiability.jl assesses rational ODEs, but practical identifiability with noisy sparse sensors, uncertainty propagation through composites and misspecification detection remain open, and no library packages "identifiable from these sensors" metadata per component ([Kapteyn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05841); [StructuralIdentifiability.jl](https://docs.sciml.ai/StructuralIdentifiability/stable/)).

### 6. Operational: IP protection, sessions and safety

The sixth problem is operational: FMU IP protection is tool-specific and "the FMI standard does not specify anything related to IP protection", which pushes a public library toward generic physics plus manufacturer parameter packs, a conclusion Mädler et al. (January 2025) reach from the process-industry side by listing IP protection, metadata standards, open formats and quality assurance as unmet requirements for exchanging equipment behaviour models ([MathWorks Answers](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/470054-how-to-protect-intellectual-property-ip-with-an-fmu-exported-from-simulink); [Mädler et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1002/ceat.202400331)); MCP has no convention for stateful sessions, units or ports, so the simulator MCP servers examined each invent their own session convention, as Ansys' PyFluent-MCP does with `connect`/`session_status` ([PyFluent-MCP](https://github.com/ansys/pyfluent-mcp)), and none of the MCP simulator servers examined offers an init/step/observe instance interface, which exists only in RL APIs like Gymnasium and OpenEnv (this is inferred from fetched tool lists; the MCP registry was not scanned) ([OpenEnv MCP lifecycle](https://huggingface.co/docs/openenv/guides/mcp-environment-lifecycle)).

Finally, the safety literature calls agentic AI "especially unsuitable for high-risk domains such as healthcare or process industry" today and ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024 is a technical report rather than a standard, so plant diagnosis must remain advisory with a human in the loop ([Flehmig et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09178)).

## Where a new library could sit: the manifest, the runtime, the data

### 1. The component manifest

The directions that survive the evidence are those that add packaging, runtime and data to physics that already exists, and each maps to a concrete, bounded engineering task rather than a research programme. The first niche is the component manifest: a JSON schema describing ports (domain, medium, potential and flow variables, causality), parameters with units, ranges, defaults and provenance, operating envelope, recommended experiment settings, canonical test scenarios and behavioural contracts, derivable automatically from Modelica annotations via OMC and from `modelDescription.xml` plus `terminalsAndIcons.xml`, SRMD and FMI-LS-REF, but assembled by no tool today.

This report proposes the minimum fields: identity with ECLASS/ETIM/IFC references, source document with revision and regulatory basis (EU 547/2012 item numbers, ISO 9906 grade), acquisition method and modifications, a 205-style performance map plus IFC/Matter-style ratings with validity range, separate data and derived-record licences, per-host reductions with residuals, and a semantic version with a source hash. The closest published requirements statement for such a manifest is Mädler et al. (Chemical Engineering & Technology 48(3), January 2025, CC-BY), which lists metadata, open formats, IP protection and quality assurance as the unmet requirements for exchanging equipment behaviour models and names AAS, CAPE-OPEN, FMI and encrypted formats as candidate solutions ([Mädler et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1002/ceat.202400331)).

The cross-walk layer should reuse existing vocabularies rather than invent one: a WoT Thing Model for the interface, IDTA 02005's port/variable/causality/flow-prefix vocabulary for the behaviour description, IFC plus bSDD, Brick/223P, SAREF4WATR, OPC UA companion and Matter device types for classification, QUDT for units, and 223P `ConnectionPoint` plus Modelica connector semantics for composition, exported to EPANET/NGSI-LD for water networks.

### 2. A robust-by-default catalogue behind an MCP server

The second niche is a robust-by-default component catalogue behind an MCP server, in which the agent selects, parameterizes and wires rather than writes. The building blocks are Buildings' valves and movers, WNTR and WaterTAP pumps behind JSON-schema tools, and `fluids` functions as typed primitives; the MCP face should use `outputSchema` and `structuredContent` for typed state, introduced in the 2025-06-18 specification and carried by the current 2026-07-28 revision (which adds a mandatory `server/discover` and a per-request protocol version), and a second `reset/step/state` face should follow OpenEnv so the same components serve RL harnesses ([MCP tools spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools); [MCP versioning](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/versioning); [OpenEnv](https://github.com/huggingface/OpenEnv)). The casys bounded-kit pattern and Device Context Protocol's range checks and dry-run evaluation, which rejected 100% of capability-escalation and 78% of prompt-injection attempts across 675 tool calls, are the safety templates to copy ([DCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26159)).

Where composition happens must be stated explicitly, because FMUs are causal black boxes and FMI leaves cross-FMU algebraic loops to the importer: the `connect` tool should operate on Modelica component instances through OMC `addComponent`/`addConnection`, run `checkModel` as the typed pre-flight, and compile the composed system to a single FMU with `buildModelFMU`, so that index reduction and the pressure-flow loop are solved inside one solver ([OMC scripting API](https://openmodelica.org/doc/OpenModelicaUsersGuide/latest/scripting_api.html)).

Pre-compiled co-simulation FMUs are reserved for causal containers that step inside a scene or plant loop, such as a valve exported with `use_p_in=true` and an embedded `Source_T`/`Sink_T` pair ([Buildings.Fluid.FMI](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_FMI_UsersGuide.html)); FMU-level composition through SSP belongs only where an SSP master that handles the coupling, OMSimulator with `addSubModel`/`addConnection` or libcosim (whose libcosimpy exposes ECCO adaptive stepping on power bonds), is part of the slice ([OMSimulator guide](https://openmodelica.org/doc/OpenModelicaUsersGuide/latest/omsimulator.html); [libcosimpy](https://github.com/open-simulation-platform/libcosimpy)). Compile at authoring time, step compiled CS FMUs at runtime, and apply non-structural parameter changes (Kv, `dp_nominal`, setpoints) through FMI `set` calls without recompilation.

### 3. Scene-side runtime and binding

The third niche is scene-side runtime and binding: a maintained FMI 3.0 co-simulation host for Unity, Unreal or Omniverse with a schema binding FMU variables to scene attributes (the unofficial `ovfmi` schema is the only sketch), a JavaScript host for FMI-LS-WASM, and a glTF KHR_interactivity profile with extension-declared hydraulic ports for cheap lumped models, all of which are unbuilt. MuJoCo's engine-plugin API is the one existing mechanism worth evaluating as an alternative to a bespoke FMI host: a faucet plugin would step inside the engine's own loop with serialized state, at the cost of a C ABI, mechanical semantics and no discovery metadata; Genesis offers liquids plus rigid bodies from Python but no component abstraction to bind to ([MuJoCo extension API](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/programming/extension.html); [Genesis](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis)).

### 4. The converter layer

The fourth niche is the converter layer, an obvious, small, high-leverage contribution: a 205-style map or Buildings `Generic` record to WNTR `add_curve`, pandapipes `PumpStdType.from_list` and an FMU parameter set, plus a bim2sim-style `IfcSanitaryTerminal` mapping and a 223P-to-`Buildings.Templates` record filler, both bounded engineering tasks.

### 5. Data

The fifth niche is data: a licence-cleared corpus of EU 547/2012-mandated pump curves with provenance, and a pump representation specification drafted in ASHRAE 205's RS0003 style (flow times head to speed and shaft power), since SSPC 205 has none.

### 6. The qualitative and discrete tier

The sixth is the qualitative and discrete tier: a statechart-plus-lumped-dynamics faucet (modes off, cold-only, hot-only, mixing, dripping) as XState/SCXML JSON with monotonic influences, which this research identifies as the most LLM-writable representation while noting that no maintained qualitative-reasoning engine exists (QSIM ships as Lisp tarballs from an FTP site; Garp3 is a SWI-Prolog GUI) ([UT QR software](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/qr/QR-software.html); [Garp3](https://ivi.fnwi.uva.nl/tcs/QRgroup/QRM/software/)).

### 7. The missing benchmark

The seventh is the missing benchmark: no study measures an agent composing multiple pre-built components, no benchmark tests unit consistency or parameter response ("double the pump speed, does head quadruple?"), and no FMU or SSP generation benchmark exists; whoever publishes one defines the category.

An open registry binding a behaviour model to an MTP service description and an AAS shell is the industrial variant of the same idea, since MTP 2.0 (20 January 2026) standardizes a module's services but, in this report's assessment, carries no information on how flow responds to pump speed, and VDI/VDE/NAMUR 2658 is being withdrawn with an objection deadline of 30 September 2026 ([PI MTP](https://www.profibus.com/technologies/mtp); [VDI 2658 Blatt 4](https://www.vdi.de/richtlinien/details/vdivdenamur-2658-blatt-4-automatisierungstechnisches-engineering-modularer-anlagen-in-der-prozessindustrie-modellierung-von-moduldiensten)).

## Recommendations: wrap the physics, build the manifest, partner for data

### Build, wrap, or partner

The build-versus-wrap-versus-partner question has a clear answer from the evidence: wrap the physics, build the packaging, partner for data and distribution. Building a new equation engine is unjustified when MSL, Buildings, WNTR and WaterTAP are actively released under BSD licences and the Python-native alternatives are dead (BondGraphTools archived 18 June 2025, PyDSTool last released 2020, pyphs 2019) ([BondGraphTools](https://github.com/BondGraphTools/BondGraphTools)); the pragmatic route for a Python-first product is Python that generates Modelica or FMUs.

Building on ModelingToolkit is possible but hazardous for a hosted service because v11 moved index reduction and tearing into AGPL packages and moved `@mtkmodel` out of core to SciCompDSL.jl, where it "will no longer receive core developer maintenance", and Dyad's terms are unconfirmed. Partnering is the right move for manufacturer data (no open API was found at Wilo, Grundfos or Xylem), for distribution (Modelon bundles Buildings 12.1.1 in Impact, water utilities buy through consultants like WSP and HDR), and for the industrial slot beneath knowledge-graph platforms that have MCP but no physics.

### Prioritized programme

The prioritized programme, each step tied to the evidence above, is as follows.

1. Priority one is to publish the component-manifest schema and a generator that emits it from OMC annotations and `modelDescription.xml`, because the one library-grounding intervention that measurably worked (PNNL's deterministic name search over the Buildings library) is a manifest by another name, and because MLQT's 600-token class view shows the token economics.
2. Priority two is the minimal viable slice: a catalogue of roughly ten robust-by-default hydraulic components (two-way and three-way valves, a mixing valve, `SpeedControlled_y` with the 18 Wilo records, a tank, a pipe, a pressure boundary) served through an MCP server with `describe_component`, `instantiate`, `set_parameter`, `connect`, `step`, `query_state` and `simulate` tools carrying `outputSchema`, with STL contracts as acceptance tests. Composition in this slice happens at the Modelica level (`connect` drives OMC `addComponent`/`addConnection`, `checkModel` gates, and the composed system compiles to one FMI 2.0 CS FMU), while stand-alone causal containers are pre-compiled for stepping in a host loop; SSP is used as the composition file only for systems whose FMU-level coupling an SSP master (OMSimulator or libcosim) is known to handle. FMI 2.0 is the packaging to promise: the 14 Buildings example FMUs are exported by Dymola scripts, OpenModelica documents its FMI 3.0.2 export as experimental, and the runtime-host evidence places full FMI 3 export in the planned 1.28.0 release, so FMI 3.0 output should be treated as pending ([OpenModelica FMI guide](https://openmodelica.org/doc/OpenModelicaUsersGuide/latest/fmitlm.html); [OpenModelica releases](https://github.com/OpenModelica/OpenModelica/releases)). The Wilo records shipped in the catalogue are illustrative, not certified: they carry no datasheet revision, test grade or data licence, so redistribution needs a licence check with Wilo or LBNL as part of priority four.
3. Priority three is a water-network agent benchmark built on WNTR and EPANET example networks that measures composition of pre-built components rather than code generation, reporting functional correctness against reference trajectories, contract violations, and tokens and wall-clock per task, using MAWB's task design and EPANET-Agentic's tool-calling baseline as templates.
4. Priority four is the EU 547/2012 curve corpus with the manifest's provenance fields, starting from the products already in Buildings and pandapipes, and a proposal for a pump representation to SSPC 205.
5. Priority five, only after the first three succeed, is the scene-side runtime: an FMI 3.0 CS host and USD or glTF binding schema, prototyped in Unity because its add-on, though frozen, has a documented import path, and evaluated on a faucet-plus-supply-plus-drain scene. The bathroom faucet should be the demonstration, not the first customer.

### What to validate first

What to validate before investing further is equally concrete.

1. First, measure whether agents given the manifest and tools compose a three-component hydraulic system correctly more often than the 24–37% functional rates of generation; if the composition rate does not clear 80% on simple systems with a frontier model, the packaging thesis is wrong.
2. Second, measure single-component solve and step times in FMPy, WNTR and WaterTAP, which no host documents, to confirm the sub-second query budget a diagnostic agent needs.
3. Third, test the data path end to end on one real pump: digitize an EU 547/2012 curve, populate the manifest, load it into all four hosts, and report the divergence between hosts; if divergence exceeds the tolerance a utility engineer accepts, the manifest needs per-host reduction residuals from day one.
4. Fourth, run three customer discovery conversations in the water sector (a consultant, a utility, a simulator vendor) to test whether they will pay for agent-callable component models or only for outcomes delivered by their existing tools; the FSC-CCF finding that integration, not models, is the top barrier means the answer may be "outcomes", in which case the library is a partner's component, not a product.
5. Fifth, check the licence and API status of Dyad and the OMEdit MCP server on Windows directly, since both are unresolved in this research and both could either shortcut or pre-empt the plan.

## Evidence limits

The recommendations above rest on a research pass whose web-search budget expired mid-way in every slice, so the following limits apply. The absence claims in this report (no FMU marketplace, no EPANET, WNTR or WaterTAP MCP server, no open manufacturer curve API, no component-composition benchmark) should be read as "not found in this research" until a targeted registry scan is run; the domain slice's own fact-check marks the MCP absence as unprovable.

`Buildings.Fluid.Movers.SpeedControlled_Nrpm` is absent from the v12.1.0 package listing, which documents only `SpeedControlled_y`, `FlowControlled_dp`, `FlowControlled_m_flow` and `Preconfigured` ([v12.1.0 Movers](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v12.1.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_Movers.html)), while the v13.0.0 User's Guide text names it alongside the other three ([v13.0.0 UsersGuide](https://simulationresearch.lbl.gov/modelica/releases/v13.0.0/help/Buildings_Fluid_Movers_UsersGuide.html)); whether it is a shipped model in 13.0.0 or only a mention in guide text was not resolved, so an agent should treat `SpeedControlled_y` as the safe choice.

The claim that AWS IoT TwinMaker is discontinued rests on a search-engine summary and is unverified: the product page, FAQ, user guide, document history (last entry 17 November 2023) and the 13 October 2025 service-availability post (which retires SiteWise Monitor and Edge packs, not TwinMaker) carry no such notice ([AWS availability post](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/10/aws-service-availability/)). Dyad's compiler licence, open-source status and external API are unstated on its pages; the OMEdit MCP server's Windows status at 1.27.1 and its tool list by name were not obtained (only the 39-tool count from a third-party issue). EPANET-Agentic's Python binding (EPyT versus WNTR) and its failure analysis sit behind a paywall and could not be read, so the 100% figure is the abstract's claim without the error breakdown.

The IDTA 02005 v1.1 date is a repository commit date; the PDF's own creation date is unverified. The Stratos25slash1to6 record's speed setting is unresolved, no host documents a single-pump solve time, and the pump-performance-db skill's data provenance is undisclosed. No source quantifies how often real IFC exports populate `Pset_PumpTypeCommon` or `Pset_ValveTypeCommon`; that fidelity problem is inferred from generator design, not measured.

OMSimulator's FMI 3.0 and SSP 2.0 support is unresolved (silent documentation, yet a layered-standard listing), and KHR_interactivity's exact ratification date is unknown. The Scenic "non-compilable code" finding was taken from a search summary of the paper's HTML version and was not re-verified against the primary page. The funding tallies (Dealroom's $3.2B) and the Gartner and McKinsey framing come from secondary or search-summary sources, and Home Assistant's licence was not checked. Finally, direct practitioner requests for such a library (Modelica Discourse, r/PLC, r/AECO, LinkedIn) were never searched, so the demand picture rests on indirect signals.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "does a world-model library exist" to "why has nobody packaged physics that has existed for twenty years so that an agent can use it", and the answer is that the value was never in the equations. Component models are free and good; what costs money and does not exist is the layer that lets a non-expert agent find the right component, trust its parameters, wire it without triggering a structural singularity, and know when the result is wrong. Every quantitative result points the same way: syntax is solved, semantic hallucination and physical inconsistency are not, and confining the agent to typed tools over verified models is the only intervention that reliably reaches 90–100%. That makes the proposed library less a modelling project than a metadata, runtime and benchmark project, closer in spirit to a package registry than to a simulator.

The implication for strategy is that the plant pump and the bathroom faucet are the same technical artifact with opposite commercial gravity. The pump has paying constituencies, an open Python substrate, a legal source of manufacturer data and an industrial ecosystem converging on MCP over knowledge graphs with an empty physics slot; the faucet has none of these and sits in scene formats that have no place to put a model. A team that ships a ten-component hydraulic catalogue with manifests, contracts and a composition benchmark, and proves that agents compose it correctly, will have built the thing every 2026 platform is missing; the faucet in the bathroom view will follow once any scene engine can host an FMU, and the same manifest will describe it.
